# Narration preparation

Prepare narration separately after content review. Keep a sourced working script, a clean spoken-text file and a pronunciation list. Production cues never enter the spoken-text file.

Choose pronunciation controls supported by the actual voice system and model. Do not put phonetic parentheticals into narration by default: they may be spoken twice. Verify names from reliable pronunciation examples when possible; mark unverified pronunciations.

Write dates, numbers and abbreviations as intended when this improves the selected engine's output. Listen to a short sample before applying broad substitutions. Homographs can often be resolved by context or rewritten naturally.

Punctuation is a hint, not a guaranteed pause duration. Check current vendor documentation before relying on dictionaries, tags, SSML or exact settings. Do not prescribe one universal voice setting or insist a whole episode fit into one generation request.

Keep voice/model/settings recorded when used; divide at natural scene boundaries under actual length limits. Listen for continuity, pronunciation and emotional restraint. Do not claim TTS-ready audio or successful listening checks when only text was inspected.
