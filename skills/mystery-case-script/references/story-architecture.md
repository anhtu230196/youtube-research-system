# Story architecture

Choose the structure after the evidence is known. Useful shapes include an investigative reversal, a long-delayed identification, a contested account, a disappearance reconstructed over time, or two investigations that converge.

Build a flexible sequence: concrete opening → enough human context → discovery → plausible questions → action and results → obstacle → consequential discovery → reinterpretation → how investigators tested the answer → outcome → remaining limits and human cost.

For a genuine false trail, document who believed it, when, why, and what challenged it. Tell it as that person's working explanation, never as an objective fact later withdrawn. Avoid premature announcements that everyone is wrong, but retain necessary attribution and exculpatory facts. A clue may merely weaken a theory; do not call that disproof.

Choose meaningful clues without a fixed count. Track their first appearance, initial interpretation, source, later interpretation, and uncertainty. Descriptions such as missing jewelry, lack of forced entry or a bite cannot alone prove identity, relationship or offender type.

A breakthrough needs a cause: a preserved sample tested, a record linked, a witness located. Explain why it emerged then. Do not replace this with an inspired hunch unless that is what sources establish.

After the discovery, reconnect the relevant earlier details, then show corroboration and consequences. Let evidence determine how much of the original event can be reconstructed. Separate a trial reconstruction from eyewitness fact. No requirement that viewers could deduce a scientific match unavailable to them.

Maintain a clear central question and useful transitions. Allow quiet human moments and resolved passages. No claim that a fixed interval, hook length or number of twists guarantees retention. Discuss institutional failures only to the degree documented; label broader interpretations. Do not infer a cover-up from delay alone.
