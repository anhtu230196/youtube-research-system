# Voice and narration

Tell the case to one attentive listener. Use clear connected paragraphs, varied sentences and short lines at earned turns. Do not force every sentence into a numerical limit. Separate legal/source annotations from the spoken experience while keeping necessary attribution in the narration.

Show a person through work, a relationship, a plan or an ordinary habit supported by evidence. Avoid stock praise, invented internal monologue and moral diagnosis. Explain technical details through the question they help answer.

Use past tense as a reliable default. Do not shift into an unobserved cinematic reconstruction merely to heighten tension. Reconstructed events remain attributed even if someone later confessed; an admission is evidence to assess, not automatic verification of every detail.

For US English narration use natural date expressions, explain unfamiliar institutions, and use measurements convenient to the audience without altering precision. Do not invent American cultural analogies. Vietnamese drafts are complete review artifacts, not English prose translated word for word.

Neutral language should preserve meaning: say that a person died or was killed when established, rather than euphemisms that obscure the event. Include a non-graphic forensic detail only if it changes an investigative conclusion. Direct audience address is optional and should help a real decision, not supply filler.

Remove stock ominous phrases and redundant re-summaries. The ending answers the opening question, states relevant limits and returns to human consequence. One optional subscribe sentence follows it.
